# Why these metrics are in the report

The report answers customer concentration, repeat-purchasing and credit-exposure questions. It separates business outcomes from evidence about whether those outcomes are interpretable.

| Business question | Useful measure | How to use it | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| How much positive transaction value remains after credits? | Gross, credits and net line value | Establish value scale and the size of adjustments; reconcile material credits before acting | Credits can relate to sales outside the period; net is not audited revenue or profit |
| How often do comparable customers purchase again? | 90-day repeated/eligible counts and rate, with quarterly detail | Compare repeat behavior with the same follow-up window | First observed purchase is not necessarily acquisition; no causal campaign attribution |
| Is value dependent on a small customer group? | Top-decile gross, credits and net versus other identified purchasers | Review account service and concentration exposure | The decile is a transparent descriptive grouping, not a loyalty score or predicted lifetime value |
| Which markets and stocks contribute the observed sales mix? | Country and merchandise-code gross rankings | Direct further investigation of market/catalog concentration | No margin, local opportunity, stock availability or reliable product taxonomy is supplied |
| What is a typical invoice, and how much do large invoices distort the mean? | Median, mean, 90th percentile and largest invoice | Ground expectations about invoice size and investigate extreme cases | Distribution context, not a revenue target or customer score |
| How representative are customer-level results? | Identified-customer share of gross value and source-quality counts | Decide how far customer findings can be generalized to anonymous purchases | Coverage is supporting evidence, not a commercial success KPI |

Removed: arbitrary RFM labels, duplicate customer quartiles, model-based reference intervals on a complete historical extract, and unequal-follow-up repeat as a headline. The 90-day headline is a ratio of pooled counts; the quarter table preserves differences that the single overall figure can hide.

Not calculated: profit, churn probability, predicted lifetime value, marketing ROI or causal impact. Cost, exposure, intervention and follow-up data needed to support those claims are absent.
