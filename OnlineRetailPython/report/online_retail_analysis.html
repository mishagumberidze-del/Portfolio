Actions to evaluate

  • Protect valuable relationships: review service needs and account coverage for the top customer decile; use net value alongside gross value because credits can change priorities.
  • Measure repeat activity consistently: use the fixed 90-day view for comparisons and evaluate a retention campaign with randomized assignment and a defined outcome window.
  • Investigate credits before changing policy: reconcile large negative lines and the matching sales with the business. Negative quantities include adjustments, and the file does not provide return reasons.
  • Improve data collection: increase customer-ID coverage and maintain a governed product catalog before using customer rankings for operational targeting.

Definitions and data quality

Gross positive line value: sum of quantity × unit price where both are positive. Returns and adjustments: absolute value of negative-quantity lines with positive price. Net line value: gross less returns/adjustments. These are transaction values, not audited revenue or profit. CU means source currency units; the CSV does not encode currency.

Observed source issues: 135,080 lines lack customer ID; 5,268 exact duplicate rows are preserved because there is no line ID in the source; 1,336 negative-quantity rows have no cancellation prefix; 2,515 lines have zero price; 2 have negative price; and 43 invoice numbers contain multiple timestamps. Negative-price and zero-price lines are excluded from gross/return/net value but retained in SQL.

Interpretation: Returns are inferred from negative quantities and may include adjustments. Customer retention is observable only for identified customers, and the one-year window right-censors late cohorts. Product ranking uses stock codes beginning with a digit and the most frequent nonblank description; the ranking can still include anomalies or non-merchandise items. Gross and net line values include service codes such as postage. No causal claims are made.
